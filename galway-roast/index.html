02 — The idea

Three origins. One coastline. Every batch turned by hand.

We roast in small batches on the west coast of Ireland — close enough to the Atlantic that the weather gets a vote. Brazilian Santos for body. Guatemalan for lift. Honduras for the long, creamy finish.

Nothing is rushed and nothing is hidden. One blend, ground and sealed within days of roasting, so what reaches your kitchen is the coffee we tasted at the drum.

05 — The ritual

Four minutes, done properly

Ground for cafetière and filter. Thirty grams to half a litre — adjust once, then keep it.

  1. 01

    Measure

    30g of ground coffee to 500ml of water. A heaped six tablespoons if the scales are elsewhere.

  2. 02

    Pour

    Just off the boil — around 94°C. Wet the grounds, wait thirty seconds for the bloom, then fill.

  3. 03

    Steep

    Four minutes for a cafetière. Lid on, plunger up, and leave it alone.

  4. 04

    Plunge

    Slowly, with the flat of your hand. Decant straight away so it stops brewing.

06 — The roastery

Small batch, west of Ireland

It started with a one-kilo roaster carried back from Turkey, a roasting course in London, and a café in Tuam that needed better coffee than it could buy. The bigger machine arrived in December 2010, and The Galway Roast has been growing ever since.

Everything is still drum roasted in quantities small enough to watch. Each batch is tasted before it is bagged — because ground coffee has a short, bright life and we would rather you spent it.
