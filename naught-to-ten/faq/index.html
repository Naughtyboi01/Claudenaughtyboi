Questions,
answered plainly.

The things people ask before they get in touch — including the ones most studios are cagey about. If yours is not here, ask it directly and you will get the same kind of answer.

Money

How much does a website cost?

Most small-business sites land between €1,000 and €2,500. A single well-made page for a trade or a small service business can come in under €1,000. Anything with a shop, online bookings or a lot of pages goes above €2,500.

You get a fixed price against a written scope before anything starts, so the number on the invoice is the number you agreed.

Why the range? What moves the price?

Mostly page count, whether you are taking payments or bookings, and whether the words and photographs already exist. A five-page site with your copy written costs meaningfully less than a five-page site where somebody has to write it.

What does hosting cost afterwards?

Usually nothing, or a few euro a month. A hand-built site is small and static, which fits inside the free tier of good hosting. You will pay for your domain — roughly €20 to €30 a year for a .ie. Both accounts are registered in your name, not ours.

How does payment work?

Half on signing, half when the site goes live, unless the proposal says otherwise. Invoices are due within fourteen days. The full terms are on the terms page.

Time

How long does it take?

Three to four weeks for a typical small-business site, from first conversation to live.

The single biggest cause of delay is content. Photographs, opening hours, staff names, the paragraph about your history nobody has written yet. Have that ready and the whole thing moves quickly.

What do you need from me to start?

A conversation, then whatever already exists: logo files, photographs, existing copy, and access to your current domain. If something important is missing we will say so at the start rather than three weeks in.

How much of my time will this take?

An hour at the start, an hour or so reviewing each stage, and however long you spend gathering content. We work asynchronously by default — you should not need to sit on calls to get a website built.

The work

Who writes the content?

Either of us. If you want to write it, you get a document listing exactly what each page needs and roughly how long it should be — far easier than staring at a blank page.

If you would rather not, we draft it from the discovery conversation and you edit until it sounds like you rather than like a brochure.

Do you do WordPress?

Only where there is a real reason to. Most small-business sites do not need a database, a login screen or monthly plugin updates — those are three ways to break or get hacked in exchange for flexibility you never use.

Hand-built pages load faster, cost less to host and have almost no attack surface. If your project genuinely needs WordPress or a shop platform, we will tell you and build it there.

Can I update it myself afterwards?

Yes. Where you need to change things regularly — prices, menus, news — we build in a simple editor so you can do it without touching code.

Where content almost never changes, we skip the editor on purpose. A content system you log into twice a year is a cost and a security risk buying you nothing.

What if I do not like the design?

You see real screens early rather than at the end, so there is no big reveal to dread. Each stage includes two rounds of revision.

If we are still not close after that, something went wrong back at the strategy stage — so we go back there and fix the thinking, rather than pushing pixels around hoping.

Will it work properly on a phone?

It is designed on a phone first, because that is where most local-business traffic arrives, and it is checked on real devices rather than a browser pretending to be one. It is also built to be keyboard-navigable and readable by a screen reader.

Afterwards

Do I own the website?

Yes. On final payment the design and code are yours. The domain and hosting accounts are in your name from day one.

There is no monthly fee to edit your own opening hours, and no version of leaving where you lose the site. If you want to move to someone else in a year, you take everything with you.

What happens to my current website?

It stays live and untouched until the new one is ready. On switch-over, every old page address is redirected to its new equivalent, so the search rankings you already have and any links pointing at you keep working.

Will it show up on Google?

The groundwork is included: page titles, descriptions, structured data, a sitemap, and loading fast enough that Google does not hold it against you.

For a local business the biggest single factor is usually a properly set-up Google Business Profile, and we will walk you through that even though it is not strictly web design. What nobody can honestly promise is a specific position — anyone who does is guessing.

Do you offer ongoing support?

Yes, but not as a compulsory retainer. Small changes are usually quick and quoted as they come up. If you would rather have a set arrangement, we can put one together — it is not a condition of the site continuing to work.

Working together

Do you work outside Galway?

Anywhere in Ireland. The work happens over calls and email either way — Galway and the west just means we can meet in person if that suits you better.

Who will I actually be dealing with?

Joshua, throughout. There is no account manager, no handover to someone junior, and nobody to hide behind. The person who quotes the work is the person who does it.

You are a new studio. Why take the risk?

A fair question, and the honest answer is that you should judge the work rather than the years. This site is the fullest example of what the studio does — you can read how it was built on the work page, and check every claim in it yourself with your browser's developer tools.

Practically: the fixed price protects you, staged payments mean you are never far out of pocket, and you own the domain and hosting from the start.
