Ten things,
in order.

Most businesses do not need all ten. The point of numbering them is that you can see where you already are, and join the climb from there. Everything below is done by one person — the one you will be talking to.

01

Discovery & Strategy

Working out what the business actually sells, and to whom, before anyone opens a design tool.

Most bad websites are not badly designed. They are badly decided. An hour of honest conversation usually surfaces the real problem — that you are competing on price when you should be competing on care, or that the thing customers value most is buried on page four.

You get a short written position: who this is for, what it promises, and what the site has to do to earn its keep. Everything after this points at that.

  • Audit of what you have
  • Customer conversations
  • Positioning
  • Written scope

02

Brand Identity

A wordmark, colours, type and tone that hold together everywhere you use them.

Not a logo on its own. A logo on its own falls apart the first time you need a van livery, a menu, an Instagram post and a footer, and none of them look related.

You get the marks as real vector files, a small set of colours that pass contrast checks, typefaces you are licensed to use, and a page of guidance short enough that you will actually read it.

  • Wordmark
  • Colour
  • Typography
  • Usage notes

03

Art Direction

How it all looks and feels before anyone reads a word.

Photography, crops, pacing, how much space things get. This is the difference between a site that looks like a template with your photos in it and one that looks like nobody else's.

Where budget does not stretch to a photographer, we will work out what you can shoot yourself on a phone and how to shoot it so it holds up.

  • Look and feel
  • Photography direction
  • Image treatment

04

UX Architecture

What goes where, in what order, and — mostly — what gets left out.

The quiet half of the work. Deciding that you need four pages instead of eleven, or that the booking button belongs above the fold on mobile because that is where three quarters of your visitors are.

You get a sitemap and the reasoning behind it, so you can disagree with the reasoning rather than the diagram.

  • Sitemap
  • Page structure
  • Key journeys

05

Interface Design

Every screen drawn properly, at every width, in every state.

Hover, focus, empty, error, loading, and the awkward one where somebody's business name is forty characters long. Designs that only work with perfect content are not designs, they are illustrations.

Drawn mobile-first, because that is where most Irish local-business traffic comes from, and checked on a real phone rather than a simulated one.

  • All key screens
  • Mobile first
  • Every state
  • Contrast checked

06

Motion & Interaction

Movement that explains something, not movement for its own sake.

A transition that shows where a panel came from is worth having. A hero that spins for two seconds before you can read anything is not. Motion also has an accessibility side: anything that moves respects a visitor's reduced-motion setting.

The scroll film on the home page of this site is the extreme end of it. Most projects want considerably less, and that is fine.

  • Scroll behaviour
  • Transitions
  • Micro-interactions
  • Reduced-motion safe

07

Front-End Engineering

The built site matching the design, to the pixel, and loading fast on a bad connection.

Hand-written HTML and CSS rather than a page builder stacking twelve nested divs to centre a heading. Semantic markup, keyboard navigable, screen-reader sane.

The result is a site that stays fast because there is not much of it, rather than one that needs a caching plugin to undo its own weight.

  • Hand-built
  • Accessible
  • Fast by construction
  • No plugin sprawl

08

Content & Copy

Words written to fit the layout, not poured into it afterwards.

The most common reason a website launches three months late is that nobody wrote the words. If you would rather write them yourself, you will get a document telling you exactly what is needed and roughly how long each piece should be.

If you would rather not, we will draft it from the discovery conversation and you edit until it sounds like you.

  • Page copy
  • Headlines
  • Editing
  • Or a brief for you to fill

09

Performance & SEO

Findable, and quick enough that people stay.

The technical groundwork: titles and descriptions that read like a human wrote them, structured data so Google understands what the business is and where it is, a sitemap, and a performance budget defended through the build rather than patched at the end.

For a local business the highest-leverage thing is usually a properly set-up Google Business Profile. That is not really web design, but we will walk you through it because it works.

  • On-page SEO
  • Structured data
  • Core Web Vitals
  • Local search setup

10

Launch & Evolution

Getting it live, handing it over properly, and staying reachable.

Domain, hosting, certificates, redirects from the old URLs so you do not lose the search rankings you already have. Then a walkthrough of how to change the things you will want to change.

You own everything. No hostage hosting, no being locked out of your own domain, no monthly fee to edit your own opening hours.

  • Deployment
  • Redirects
  • Handover
  • You own it all
